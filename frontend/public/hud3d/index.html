    HOW THE GENETICS SIMULATOR WORKS

    HOW BEYOND HORIZONS WORKS

    Beyond Horizons is a health-monitoring console for astronauts on long missions. It watches the crew member's vital signs, the habitat around them and long-term spaceflight risks, shows where in the body a problem is, and explains every alert with the NASA evidence behind it.

    OVERVIEW

    Live vital signs, habitat environment, body scan, genetics, systems status and AI insights. Readings turn amber (watch) or red (alert) outside their limits. The RADIATION tile follows the situation chosen in GENETICS (ISS 0.2–0.5, deep space 1.8 mSv/day) and the solar-event dose you set there; click it to open GENETICS. View Details explains each flagged reading: value, normal range, what it can mean, which organ lights up and what to do.

    ASTRONAUT / SCAN MODE

    Scan mode turns the suit into a hologram and reveals the organs. Systems with a problem glow and are labelled on the body (DIGESTIVE (GI) = stomach and intestines). Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, right-drag to move.

    SCENARIOS

    Pick a situation (hypoxia, arrhythmia, solar storm, heat stress, bone loss, SANS…) or create your own with any readings: a preview shows what the scan will flag, and an optional mission day puts it on the MISSION timeline. Keys 1–9 switch scenarios.

    BIOMETRICS

    Beating 3D heart and breathing lungs coloured by status, trends of the mission feed or of your own saved history, and the reference ranges used.

    MY VITALS

    Enter your own readings. They are evaluated, saved to your history, charted over time and can be shown on the hologram. Export/import your history as a file.

    GENETICS

    Choose a situation (ISS, one year in space, Mars transit), days, a solar event and countermeasures: see the radiation dose, which genes change and by how much (published NASA-funded data), the consequences NASA found, and the affected markers lighting up on the DNA.

    CREW RISK

    Simulates a 4-person, 180-day Mars-transit crew day by day. Click EDIT CREW to rename the crew and set their 6 traits, turn mission events on/off, choose destination, duration and countermeasures, and see who is at risk, when and why. Dashed lines mark the current mission day and the EVA day.

    EVA DECISION

    An EVA is a spacewalk. On day 145 one is planned just after a solar storm (day 143). Option A: keep it; B: delay 10 days; C: give it to a rested crew member. The tab compares their success chance and risks, and four roles debate which to pick. The MISSION LINK box shows how many days until the spacewalk, each crew member's risk on day 145 (your crew) and whether the astronaut on the Overview is GO / CAUTION / NO-GO.

    RESEARCH

    Evidence graph: how radiation, microgravity, sleep and workload lead to risk, with each link labelled by how strong its evidence is (14 factors, 13 links). Click a box or a line to see its source and limitations. Plus the next experiments worth doing.

    MISSION

    Choose a mission profile and move day by day (or press play): the ship moves in 3D and you see the phase, the radiation received so far against NASA's career limit, and the events. Click a timeline item to jump to that day. With Astronaut status follows the mission on, events change the Overview scan (solar storm → radiation, exercise failure → bone loss) and your own scenarios apply on their day.

    BEYOND AI

    Ask questions about the crew, the current scan, your vitals or the evidence. Answers use only this platform's data.

    NASA SOURCES

    Every green tag opens its source: NASA-STD-3001, ISS and Curiosity radiation measurements, HRP evidence reports, the Twins Study, OSDR/GeneLab datasets.
